Vibration FFT planning

Choose a recording length and inspect frequency-bin spacing for a vibration measurement.

Example: 4,096 samples at 25,600 Hz give 6.25 Hz bin spacing, a 0.16-second record and a 12,800 Hz Nyquist frequency.

How it works & model limitations

FFT bin spacing = sample rate ÷ number of acquired samples. Record duration = samples ÷ sample rate. Nyquist frequency = sample rate ÷ 2.

Bin spacing is not guaranteed separation of nearby tones. Window choice, leakage, noise and stationarity affect useful resolution. Zero-padding does not improve the physical resolution of the acquired record. Use a suitable anti-alias filter and leave a transition band below Nyquist; a frequency below Nyquist alone does not establish measurement validity. This planner does not analyze a vibration waveform or diagnose a machine.

How many vibration samples are needed for 1 Hz FFT bins?

At 25,600 samples per second, a 4,096-sample record lasts 0.16 seconds and gives 6.25 Hz bin spacing. To obtain 1 Hz bins at the same sample rate, collect 25,600 samples over one second.

Try a different scenario

Increasing the acquired record length narrows bin spacing, but changing speed during the record can smear vibration features. Zero-padding only interpolates the spectrum. Select a window and anti-alias filter appropriate to the measurement.

Common questions

Does zero-padding improve frequency resolution?

Zero-padding adds interpolated spectral points without lengthening the measured record. It does not supply new information to separate nearby tones. Here, sample count means acquired samples.

Is staying below Nyquist enough?

No. An anti-alias filter needs a transition band, and noise, windowing, leakage and changing speed affect interpretation. The planner reports sampling quantities; it does not diagnose machine condition.
