01

Battery lifetime

Find out how active bursts and sleep current affect an IoT device’s battery budget.

Example: a 2,000 mAh battery with 80% usable capacity, a 20 mA burst for 1 second every 60 seconds and 10 µA sleep current gives approximately 194 days.

How it works & model limitations

Average current = active current × duty cycle + sleep current × (1 − duty cycle). Lifetime = usable capacity ÷ average current.

Use battery-side currents, including regulator and peripheral consumption. This model assumes a repeating cycle and constant usable capacity. It does not predict self-discharge, pulse-related voltage sag, temperature effects or aging. The usable-capacity percentage is your assumption, not an automatic chemistry correction.

02

Camera motion blur

Estimate image smear and choose an exposure time for a moving subject.

Example: 200 mm across 2,000 pixels, at 1,000 mm/s and 100 µs exposure, gives 1 pixel of motion blur. A 2-pixel limit permits up to 200 µs.

How it works & model limitations

Scale = field of view ÷ image width. Blur = speed × exposure ÷ scale. Maximum exposure = allowed blur × scale ÷ speed.

Assumes constant lateral movement in the object plane. Use the actual field of view and output pixel width after ROI, resizing or binning. It does not model rolling-shutter distortion, vibration, defocus, diffraction or lighting. Zero speed has no motion-based exposure limit in this model.

03

Supercapacitor hold-up

Estimate how long a capacitor bank can support a constant-power load.

Example: 10 F from 5 V down to 3 V stores 80 J in the usable voltage window. At 80% efficiency and a 4 W load, estimated hold-up is 16 seconds.

How it works & model limitations

Stored energy = ½ × C × (start voltage² − minimum voltage²). Hold-up time = stored energy × efficiency ÷ load power.

Enter effective bank capacitance, not individual cell capacitance for a series bank. Efficiency is treated as constant. ESR voltage drop, converter current limits, startup, leakage, balancing losses, aging and temperature are not modeled. Set minimum voltage to reflect the actual converter and load requirements. Validate with the component datasheets and a measured discharge test.

04

AI camera storage

Plan recording capacity from the measured encoded bitrate of your camera streams.

Example: four 4 Mbps cameras recording 24 hours/day for 7 days produce 1.2096 TB of video; adding 20% overhead gives 1.45152 TB.

How it works & model limitations

Daily GB = cameras × Mbps × 0.45 × recording hours. Required TB = daily GB × retention days × (1 + overhead / 100) ÷ 1000. GB and TB use decimal units.

Enter the measured encoded bitrate, including audio if recorded. AI processing does not determine bitrate. Variable bitrate, motion-triggered recording, metadata, filesystem capacity and RAID reduce predictability. Overhead is an added allowance, not a percentage of disk space reserved. Aggregate bitrate assumes all cameras record simultaneously; network protocol overhead is excluded.

05

Edge AI frame-time budget

Compare measured processing times against a target frame rate before choosing your next optimization.

Example: 10 + 5 + 20 + 5 ms gives 40 ms/frame and a serial processing limit of 25 FPS. A 30 FPS target has a −6.66667 ms time margin.

How it works & model limitations

Serial frame time = capture + preprocessing + inference + postprocessing. Compute-limited FPS = 1000 ÷ serial frame time. Time margin = 1000 ÷ target FPS − serial frame time.

Assumes one frame at a time with no overlapping stages and no batching. Use representative measured times on the target hardware. This is not a model benchmark or a prediction from TOPS. Camera frame-rate limits, queueing, transfer overhead, jitter and thermal throttling may reduce actual throughput. Include transfers in the measured stage times. Pipelined throughput needs a separate model.

06

Vibration FFT planning

Choose a recording length and inspect frequency-bin spacing for a vibration measurement.

Example: 4,096 samples at 25,600 Hz give 6.25 Hz bin spacing, a 0.16-second record and a 12,800 Hz Nyquist frequency.

How it works & model limitations

FFT bin spacing = sample rate ÷ number of acquired samples. Record duration = samples ÷ sample rate. Nyquist frequency = sample rate ÷ 2.

Bin spacing is not guaranteed separation of nearby tones. Window choice, leakage, noise and stationarity affect useful resolution. Zero-padding does not improve the physical resolution of the acquired record. Use a suitable anti-alias filter and leave a transition band below Nyquist; a frequency below Nyquist alone does not establish measurement validity. This planner does not analyze a vibration waveform or diagnose a machine.
