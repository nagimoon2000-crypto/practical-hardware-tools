RADIO RETRIES AND SENSOR BATTERY LIFE
One-hour bench worksheet | Practical Hardware Tools | 7 November 2026

Question: how much battery life do measured radio retries cost?
Compare two hours of operation with the same number of scheduled reports.
Use hours representative of the deployment. Record failed attempts too.

Worked example
60 scheduled reports in 3,600 seconds; 12 extra attempts; each attempt
uses 50 mA for 0.40 s. Between attempts the device draws 20 microamps.
The 72 attempts occupy 28.8 s. Sleep occupies the remaining 3,571.2 s.
Total charge = (50 x 28.8 + 0.020 x 3,571.2) / 3,600 = 0.41984 mAh.
Over one hour this corresponds to 0.41984 mA average.
Without the 12 retries: 24 s active and 3,576 s asleep = 0.35320 mAh.
For a 1,000 mAh battery with 80% usable capacity, the estimates are
79.4 days with retries and 94.4 days without them. These are modeled
lifetimes under the stated duty cycles, not field measurements.

Bench checks
[ ] Measure at the battery so that both states include regulator losses.
[ ] Sum actual awake durations if retries use different currents or lengths.
[ ] Count listening, acknowledgments and backoff separately if they are
    missing from your active trace. Check that state times sum to the window.
[ ] Compare normal reception and weak reception separately. Record drops.
[ ] Check peak current, battery sag and cutoff voltage before accepting
    the estimate. Averages alone do not establish that the device will run.

Optional: record multiple operating states in the battery planning pack.
