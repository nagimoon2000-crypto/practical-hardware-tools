Separate pipeline throughput from the latency of one frame

A camera pipeline may process different frames in different stages at the same time. Adding stage durations estimates one serial path, while sustained output rate depends on scheduling and resource contention.

All tools and notes

Compare two explicit models

Take stages lasting 8 ms capture, 4 ms preprocessing, 16 ms inference and 2 ms postprocessing. With no overlap, the sum is 30 ms and the serial limit is 33.333 FPS. With perfectly independent stages and unlimited buffering, an ideal steady-state pipeline can complete a frame every 16 ms, or 62.5 FPS. Its first frame still takes 30 ms through these stages before any queueing.

Do not treat the ideal bound as a benchmark

Shared CPU threads, transfers, a single accelerator, synchronization and camera cadence can prevent perfect overlap. Batching can also improve throughput while increasing waiting time. The existing calculator deliberately reports the serial estimate; do not reinterpret its result as a concurrent-pipeline prediction.

Measure two quantities

For throughput, divide completed frames by a steady observation interval and record dropped frames. For latency, correlate a frame identifier from capture to completed output, including queue time, and inspect the distribution rather than only the mean. Document warm-up, input rate, batch size and transfers. NVIDIA’s benchmarking guidance distinguishes throughput and latency metrics; an inference-only tool result should not be labelled camera-to-result performance.
