Recognize an aliased vibration tone before interpreting an FFT peak

Fine FFT bins cannot rescue a signal that was aliased at acquisition. Check the analog signal bandwidth and filtering before interpreting a low-frequency peak as a real mechanical component.

All tools and notes

Work through a folded tone

At a sample rate of 1,000 samples/s, Nyquist frequency is 500 Hz. An unfiltered 700 Hz sine can appear at 300 Hz because |700 − 1,000| = 300. Acquiring 2,000 samples gives a two-second record and 0.5 Hz bin spacing, but that finer spacing still places the aliased tone near 300 Hz.

Use the calculator for the right check

Enter 1,000 Hz sampling, 2,000 acquired samples and a highest frequency of interest of 700 Hz. The Nyquist check should reject that acquisition plan for ordinary baseband measurement. Changing only the sample count changes duration and bin spacing; it does not change the 500 Hz Nyquist limit.

Check the acquisition chain

Inspect the sensor and analog filter response, including energy above your band of interest. Choose a sample rate and anti-alias filtering with room for a real filter transition band; merely sampling at exactly twice the desired edge is not a practical filter design. Investigate suspect peaks with a controlled change of acquisition settings. Once frequencies have folded together in the recorded data, a later digital filter cannot generally determine which original tone produced them.
